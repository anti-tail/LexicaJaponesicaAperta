Appendix: ONCOJ annotation compared with the CHJ Nara Period Series and with dictionaries of Classical Japanese

This appendix compares how the two annotated corpora of Old Japanese are annotated: the Oxford-NINJAL Corpus of Old Japanese (ONCOJ) and the Nara Period Series of NINJAL's Corpus of Historical Japanese (CHJ). The main difference is that ONCOJ puts constituency trees with grammatical functions on top of the original script and a phonemic transcription, whereas CHJ puts UniDic-based morphological information on a kanji-kana reading text and has no syntactic annotation. ONCOJ also treats auxiliaries as bound morphemes inside the word and counts -ba, -te, -tutu etc. as inflected forms, while CHJ segments them as separate words.

Section 9 is for readers who look words up in a dictionary of Classical Japanese (kogo jiten) before searching ONCOJ: it explains how lemmas and word boundaries differ, e.g. that verbs are listed under the infinitive, that there is no auxiliary kemu or zi, and that some no, ni and to are a copula.

4. Inflected forms

In CHJ mi-gohō is an inflected form, and the form with mi is one Long Unit. ONCOJ represents it as an infinitive ADJ-INF with the adjectival copula -mi (L000034, ACP-INF) inside (470 instances).

5. Auxiliaries and particles

  • ONCOJ distinguishes auxiliaries by a functional component on VAX: CJR conjectural (-m-), NEG negative, PRF perfective (-n-, -te-), SPST simple past (-ki-), MPST modal past (-kyer-), STV stative (-er-, -tar-), PASS passive, CTV causative, RSP respect (-s-), SJV subjunctive (-masi); the inflected form comes last (VAX-CJR-ADC). Lemmas are IDs such as L000002 (-m-).
  • CHJ treats each auxiliary as an independent Short Unit Word of the POS 助動詞; ki, keri, nu, tu, mu, ramu, zu etc. get their own conjugation types (e.g. 文語助動詞-ズ), with the same inflected forms as other inflecting words.
  • Particles: ONCOJ subdivides case particles by case (ABL, ACC, ALL, COM, DAT, GEN, NOM) and final particles by meaning (DES desiderative, EVD evidential, MPH emphatic, PHB prohibitive, XCL exclamative). CHJ has six classes (case, adverbial, binding, conjunctional, final, nominalizing); case and meaning are not annotated (they are told apart by lemma).
  • Prohibitive na…so: in the current ONCOJ annotation na is an adverb ADV and so a final particle P-FNL-PHB (circumfix CFX-PHB in 2021).

6. Syntactic annotation

CHJ has no syntactic annotation. Long Units are cut out after identifying bunsetsu, so the bunsetsu concept is used for the units, but no dependency relations or labels are given. There is research adding kakari-musubi information to CHJ texts including the Man'yōshū, and part of that data has been released (see Sources).

ONCOJ has constituency trees, whose node labels also carry grammatical functions, semantic roles and clause types.

9. How ONCOJ differs from a dictionary of Classical Japanese

If you look a word up in a dictionary of Classical Japanese (kogo jiten) and then search for it in ONCOJ, the headword form and the way the word is segmented often differ, and the word may seem to be missing. Kogo dictionaries follow the Japanese school grammar: they list verbs and adjectives under the conclusive form and treat each auxiliary and particle as a separate word. ONCOJ segments morphemes and sets up its lemmas following Frellesvig's analysis. This section lists the differences that most often get in the way. Counts are from the working data taken on 2026-11-18 (occurrences, unless they are said to be dictionary entries).

9.1 Verbs are listed under the infinitive

The ONCOJ dictionary lists verbs under the infinitive (ren'yōkei). The quadrigrade verb omofu ‘think’ is omopi, the lower bigrade wasuru ‘forget’ is wasure, whatever the conjugation class.

Auxiliaries follow the same principle: perfective nu is listed as ni, tu as te, respect su as si, passive yu as ye, and simu as sime (9.3).

Adjectives: for KU-adjectives the lemma is the bare stem (takasi ‘high’ → taka L007005, yosi ‘good’ → yo L007015); for SIKU-adjectives it runs up to si (kurusi ‘painful’ → kurusi L007096). The endings -ki, -si, -ku are the adjectival copula ACP (L000033), a separate node inside the adjective node. In the conclusive of a SIKU-adjective the ending is empty (NULL), and the kar- of the kari-conjugation is a lemma of its own, L000032.

If you type a conclusive form in kana (おもふ, わする, しぬ) into the “Examples of a word” box, lemmas that differ only in the last syllable are offered as well, so you reach the infinitive lemma. みる, however, hits the seaweed miru, and す and く hit other words. For upper monograde, S-irregular and K-irregular verbs, type the infinitive (み, し, き) or the kanji (見る, 為, 来).

9.2 Quadrigrade stems and stative -yer-

ONCOJ cuts quadrigrade verb stems as consonant-final (nok-, pum-). A kogo dictionary divides nokoreru ‘remaining’ into the exclamatory (imperative) form nokore and the adnominal ru of the auxiliary ri; ONCOJ divides it into the stem nok and the stative auxiliary yeru (L000024, lemma yeri). The boundary is shifted by one vowel, so search for ri by its lemma number.

(VB-ADN (VB-STM (L031371a (PHON nok))) (VAX-STV-ADN (L000024 (PHON yeru))))   nokyeru   Bussokuseki-ka 5

L000024 occurs 711 times. The difference between yeri and eri depends on the preceding consonant: after k, g, p, b, m, where kō and otsu e are distinguished, it is yeri (written like the kō syllable in kyeri); after r, s, t etc., where the distinction is neutralized, it is eri (e.g. nok-yeru, tat-eru). According to the dictionary note, the form ari is Eastern Old Japanese. For how STM relates to the school-grammar forms, see STM in section 4.

9.5 Subject ga and no

Kogo dictionaries give ga and no a nominative use, marking the subject of adnominal and conditional clauses, besides the genitive use. In ONCOJ the particle tag depends on the form: ga (L000503) and no (L000520) stay P-CASE-GEN (genitive) when they mark a subject. Subjecthood is shown by the phrase label PP-SBJ.

(PP-SBJ (NP (N (L050090 (PHON pi)))) (P-CASE-GEN (L000503 (PHON ga))))   pi ga   Kojiki song 3

Of the 4,202 PP-SBJ nodes, 2,145 end in a genitive particle (structure query PP-SBJ < P-CASE-GEN). Nominative P-CASE-NOM is only i, 9 times.

9.6 Conjunctional particles and the ku-nominal

What kogo dictionaries call the conjunctional particles ba, do, domo, te, tutu, and the ku of the ku-nominal (ku-gohō), are parts of inflected forms in ONCOJ (section 4). The mateba of tuki mateba is one word, the provisional VB-PRV; there is no word ba on its own. To find forms with ba, search the inflection component, e.g. /-(CND|PRV)$/ in a structure query, or /-GER$/ for te.

9.7 Kō/otsu and the transcription

Lemmas and phonetic forms are in the Frellesvig–Whitman transcription. The h-row is p, and the kō (A) syllables of jōdai tokushu kanazukai are written with y or w (kye, kwo, pwi) to distinguish them from the otsu (B) syllables: keri is kyeri, kofu is kwopwi. If you type kana, the “Examples of a word” box ignores kō/otsu, and the “Words” search mode ignores both kō/otsu and voicing, so you can search without knowing the transcription.
