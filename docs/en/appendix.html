Appendix: ONCOJ annotation compared with the CHJ Nara Period Series and with the grammars of Vovin and Frellesvig

This appendix compares how the two annotated corpora of Old Japanese are annotated: the Oxford-NINJAL Corpus of Old Japanese (ONCOJ; Frellesvig, Horn et al. 2026) and the Nara Period Series of NINJAL's Corpus of Historical Japanese (CHJ). The main difference is that ONCOJ puts constituency trees with grammatical functions on top of the original script and a phonemic transcription, whereas CHJ puts UniDic-based morphological information on a kanji-kana reading text and has no syntactic annotation. ONCOJ also treats auxiliaries as bound morphemes inside the word and counts -ba, -te, -tutu etc. as inflected forms, while CHJ segments them as separate words.

Section 9 is for readers who come to ONCOJ from the English-language reference grammars: Frellesvig's A History of the Japanese Language (2010), whose analysis ONCOJ follows, and Vovin's Descriptive and Comparative Grammar of Western Old Japanese (first edition 2005–2009, second edition 2020). It sets the transcriptions and the grammatical terms side by side, and explains how lemmas and word boundaries are set up: verbs are listed under the infinitive, there is no lemma for kemu or zi, and some no, ni and to are a copula. The Japanese version of this appendix compares ONCOJ with dictionaries of Classical Japanese (kogo jiten) instead.

In CHJ mi-gohō is an inflected form, and the form with mi is one Long Unit. ONCOJ represents it as an infinitive ADJ-INF with the adjectival copula -mi (L000034, ACP-INF) inside (470 instances). When to follows, as in yama wo sagasi mito, ONCOJ writes mito as one word, ACP-GER (39 occurrences).

5. Auxiliaries and particles

  • ONCOJ distinguishes auxiliaries by a functional component on VAX: CJR conjectural (-m-), NEG negative, PRF perfective (-n-, -te-), SPST simple past (-ki-), MPST modal past (-kyer-), STV stative (-er-, -tar-), PASS passive, CTV causative, RSP respect (-s-), SJV subjunctive (-masi); the inflected form comes last (VAX-CJR-ADC). Lemmas are IDs such as L000002 (-m-).
  • CHJ treats each auxiliary as an independent Short Unit Word of the POS 助動詞; ki, keri, nu, tu, mu, ramu, zu etc. get their own conjugation types (e.g. 文語助動詞-ズ), with the same inflected forms as other inflecting words.
  • Particles: ONCOJ subdivides case particles by case (ABL, ACC, ALL, COM, DAT, GEN, NOM) and final particles by meaning (DES desiderative, EVD evidential, MPH emphatic, PHB prohibitive, XCL exclamative). CHJ has six classes (case, adverbial, binding, conjunctional, final, nominalizing); case and meaning are not annotated (they are told apart by lemma).
  • Prohibitive na…so: in the current ONCOJ annotation na is an adverb ADV and so a final particle P-FNL-PHB (circumfix CFX-PHB in 2021).

6. Syntactic annotation

CHJ has no syntactic annotation. Long Units are cut out after identifying bunsetsu, so the bunsetsu concept is used for the units, but no dependency relations or labels are given. There is research adding kakari-musubi information to CHJ texts including the Man'yōshū, and part of that data has been released (see Sources).

ONCOJ has constituency trees, whose node labels also carry grammatical functions, semantic roles and clause types.

9. ONCOJ and the grammars of Vovin and Frellesvig

ONCOJ follows the analysis of Old Japanese in Frellesvig (2010, ch. 3): the tag names are abbreviations of his terms, and the transcription is the Frellesvig–Whitman system used in that book. A reader of Frellesvig will therefore find the same categories, with a few differences noted below. Vovin's grammar (first edition: Part 1, 2005, and Part 2, 2009; second edition 2020) describes the same forms with a different transcription, different names and, in places, a different segmentation. His Reference Grammar of Classical Japanese Prose (2003) uses the terms of the first edition (infinitive, subordinative gerund, final, attributive, evidential, tentative, progressive, perfective-progressive, hearsay) for the language of Heian prose, where the kō/otsu distinction no longer applies. Counts are from the working data taken on 2026-11-18 (occurrences, unless they are said to be dictionary entries).

Points where ONCOJ departs from Frellesvig (2010): the prohibitive -una is not an inflected form but a final particle na after the conclusive (P-FNL-PHB), and the na of na … so is an adverb (ADV). Points where Vovin differs in substance: the iterative -ap- is a suffix for Vovin, whereas ONCOJ lists verbs such as kakusapi ‘hide’ or katarapi ‘talk’ as lemmas of their own, linked to the base verb in the dictionary; Vovin's exclamative -umǝ is the final particle mo in ONCOJ (P-FNL-MPH, 26); his potential -kate-, negative potential -kane- and benefactive -kǝse- auxiliaries are verbs in ONCOJ (VB: kate L030495a, kane L030505a, kose L031964a). Verb classes: ONCOJ and Frellesvig use quadrigrade, upper and lower bigrade, upper monograde and the irregular classes; Vovin speaks of consonant verbs, vowel verbs and irregular verbs.

9.3 Verbs are listed under the infinitive

The ONCOJ dictionary lists verbs under the infinitive (ren'yōkei), not under the conclusive of the Japanese dictionaries or the stem used by Vovin and Frellesvig (omop-, wasure-). The quadrigrade verb ‘think’ is omopi, the lower bigrade ‘forget’ is wasure, whatever the conjugation class.

9.4 Quadrigrade stems and stative -yer-

Like Frellesvig and Vovin, ONCOJ cuts quadrigrade (consonant) verb stems before the vowel (nok-, pum-), and attaches the stative auxiliary to that stem: Frellesvig's stative -yer-, Vovin's progressive -er-. The school grammar divides nokeru ‘has left behind’ into the form noke and the adnominal ru of the auxiliary ri; ONCOJ divides it into the stem nok and yeru (L000024, lemma yeri).

(VB-ADN (VB-STM (L031371a (PHON nok))) (VAX-STV-ADN (L000024 (PHON yeru))))   nok yeru   Bussokuseki-ka 5

L000024 occurs 711 times. The difference between yeri and eri depends on the preceding consonant: after k, g, p, b, m, where kō and otsu e are distinguished, it is yeri (written like the kō syllable in kyeri); after r, s, t etc., where the distinction is neutralized, it is eri (e.g. nok-yeru, tat-eru). According to the dictionary note, the form ari is Eastern Old Japanese. Stems before auxiliaries are tagged STM (section 4).

9.6 The copulas n- and t-

Following Frellesvig (2010, 3.3), ONCOJ analyses part of what are traditionally the particles ni, no, to, tu, the auxiliary nari and the suffix nasu as the copula COP, under three lemmas. Vovin has the same forms as defective verbs: n- ‘to be’ with the converb n-i, the attributive n-ǝ and the special form nar-, and tǝ with the converb tǝ and the attributive t-u. His comparative case marker -nǝ / -ni / -nasu / -nǝsu corresponds to ONCOJ's COP-SEM and to part of COP-ADI no and COP-INF ni; his genitive-locative case marker -tu corresponds to P-CASE-GEN tu (oki tu …).

9.7 Case and other particles

ONCOJ treats case markers as particles (P-CASE); Vovin treats them as case suffixes of the noun. The tags name the case by the form: ga (L000503), no (L000520) and tu are all P-CASE-GEN, also when ga or no marks a subject. Subjecthood is shown by the phrase label PP-SBJ.

(PP-SBJ (NP (N (L050090 (PHON pi)))) (P-CASE-GEN (L000503 (PHON ga))))   pi ga   Kojiki song 3

Of the 4,202 PP-SBJ nodes, 2,145 contain a genitive particle (structure query PP-SBJ < P-CASE-GEN). Nominative P-CASE-NOM is only i, 9 times.
